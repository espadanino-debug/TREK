# First $100K (individual, pre-revenue or broke)

Source: "If I Wanted to Make My First $100K in 2026, I'd Do This" (jfW6gL6hKhk) transcript. Goal is $100K *banked*, not revenue: "be rich, not look rich."

Six steps:

1. **Cut all money costs to take more risk.** No eating out, discount groceries only, current clothes for two years, live with family or share a room (he split a bedroom paying $300–400/mo even at $20K/mo income), paid-off cheap car.
2. **Cut time costs.** Your 9–5 isn't killing your dreams; your wasted 5–9 a.m. and 5–9 p.m. are. Focus is achieved by subtraction.
   - **4-4-4 split** (when self-employed): 4 h promote (first thing: let people know about your stuff), 4 h deliver, 4 h build (curate opportunities, then prioritise the one with highest return).
   - **Maker vs. manager**: protect a block of maker time (his: first 4–6 h of the day; Mondays are manager day). Task switching kills output.
3. **Research a skill people already pay for.** B2B: every function a business pays for (content, outreach, funnels, ads…) can be a $1M+ business. B2C: print your own bank statement and see what you buy. Then the **1-1-1 rule**: one product, one avatar, one channel until $1M.
4. **Learn properly.** Learning = same conditions, new behaviour. If what you consume doesn't change what you do, it isn't learning. "10,000 iterations, not 10,000 hours" – needs feedback loops. Method: do volume → pull the top 10% of results → find what they share that the other 90% lack → isolate the details that matter (test one at a time) → repeat 100 more reps. Fastest path: hire someone great one-on-one.
5. **Spend on the right three buckets:** tools (CRM, landing pages, community software), implementation help (courses, communities, tutoring), trial attempts (ad spend, editing software).
6. **Increase active income, don't increase lifestyle.** Self-made billionaires had monster *active* income to take risk with. "The day you stop spending on learning is the day you decide you don't want to make more."

Why it matters: $100K saved was the moment he felt wealthiest – the first time he could think long-term instead of about rent.
