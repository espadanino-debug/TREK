# Offers and pricing

Sources: transcripts of "If I Started A Business in 2026, I'd Do This" (uWdIgftpvBI) and "What Makes The Perfect Business" (3fsJFUvA6Ts); value equation formula as published in *$100M Offers*.

## Value equation

Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood of achievement) ÷ (Time delay × Effort & sacrifice)

Raise the top, shrink the bottom. Working each lever:

- **Dream outcome** – describe the *right avatar's* pain more precisely than they can. "Pain and persuasion only exist in the specific, never the vague." If you articulate the problem better than the prospect, they assume you can solve it. Tactic: mine reviews of the best-selling books in the niche for exact pain language.
- **Perceived likelihood** – reputation acts as an implied guarantee; one-on-one delivery, proof, case studies and guarantees raise it. They must believe you're (1) competent and (2) intend to help.
- **Time delay** – the strongest lever. "Latency beats magnitude." Wealthy buyers pay more to save time than to save money. Cut delivery time by ½–⅔; promise priority, first-in-line, emergency response.
- **Effort & sacrifice** – walk the customer journey, list every action the customer must take, and delete friction points one by one. The more expensive, the more done-for-you it must be.

## Sell premium first ("the middle is where people die")

Either sell very expensive to a few or very cheap to everyone. Starting out, expensive-to-few is easier (Tesla Roadster → S → 3).

Why a capped, unscalable 1:1 / premium tier is worth it:
- Learn faster from fewer, higher-value clients; iterate delivery on the fly.
- Shifts your belief about what the market will pay.
- ~100% margin on your own time; funds growth of the scalable product.
- Supply is fixed → "when you have demand, cut supply" → forces price up.
- Lifts the whole brand: the high anchor transfers perceived value to the cheaper tier, and "my private clients" makes great marketing and case studies.

**The math to show users:** 100 buyers, $100 product at 40% margin, plus a $1,000 tier. If 10 take the premium: 90 × $40 = $3,600 profit from the base vs. 10 × $1,000 = $10,000 from the premium. Revenue doubles; ~¾ of profit comes from 10% of buyers.

**Action step:** list the premium price wherever you sell and *say it out loud* on offers. You must confront the price: "Would you like to work with me one-on-one? It's $10K." Then stop talking. If they balk: "I'll pull out the components that matter most into this version that has 90% of it for a fraction." Every business can have ~5 one-on-one clients (e.g. lawn care: owner's cell vs. account rep's line; owner designs the garden).

**Three frames to design the premium tier:**
1. If they paid 10× or 100× what you charge now, what would you include? List it, cross out items with hard costs, offer what's left.
2. If word of mouth from this one customer were your *only* source of new customers, what would the experience have to be?
3. Strip out everything unscalable, but it must still be worth 10× – how?

Pick the right avatar for the premium tier: it's usually a *different person* than your $100 buyer (has money, feels the pain, easy to reach).

## Speed incentives (pure margin)

If you're supply-constrained, sell the front of the line. Same work, reordered queue, more money. Example: "20% priority boost: done by end of week or it's free." Roofing case: an extra $300 to do it tomorrow instead of next week was taken and led to the next job.

## Decommoditize

Commodities have thin margins (grocery, restaurants, farming). Branding, bundling, guarantees, speed and a named unique process ("our Special K process" vs. "the old way") let you charge more for the same core thing.
