# Sales

Sources: "After Closing 4000+ Sales, I Discovered a New Method to Close Deals Faster" (RVbvhPGFi6E) transcript; menu close from o64cI6tebnU. For depth, point users to "The Ultimate Sales Training for 2026" (StVqS0jD7Ls) and "How to Sell Better than 99% Of People" (JE2_7elAcxM). (The CLOSER framework from his sales trainings: Clarify, Label, Overview past pain, Sell the vacation, Explain away concerns, Reinforce. That list is recalled from his public material, not one of these transcripts.)

## Principle

The top closers **ask for the sale more times** than anyone. That only works if each ask *keeps rapport*, so you need to reframe before every close attempt.

**Reframe** = the 1–3 sentences after a prospect says anything but yes, which raise the odds that your next line gets the yes.

## 3A framework

1. **Acknowledge** – repeat what they said. Shows listening, buys you thinking time.
2. **Associate** – link their question to how your *best* customers behave: "Great question – actually our most successful clients ask that." Now their step away becomes a step toward buying. Give them a label (smart, rational, family man) you can bring back at the close.
3. **Ask** – a question about their question, not an answer.

Example: "What certifications do your trainers have?" → "So you're curious about certifications – great question, shows you're taking this seriously. Which certifications are you looking for specifically?"

## Five rules

1. **The person asking questions is in control.** Prospects believe little of what *you* say and almost everything *they* say: breadcrumb them to say "I think I'm a good fit." Never ask "Do you have any questions?" Handling "why won't you answer?": surgeon/secretary or mechanic analogy – "it'd be unethical to diagnose without looking under the hood; anyone who answers before an assessment is just telling you what you want to hear."
2. **Nobody can disagree with a question.** "Be like smoke." Never voice disagreement; you never win a sale by winning an argument.
3. **Tell them what their question means** (the association). "That's great – 90% of clients who switch to us already had an advisor, so you'll be ahead of the learning curve."
4. **Use straw men for tough truths**, so you never insult the prospect directly:
   - Someone else: "Funny, someone asked me that earlier today – can I share what I told them?"
   - A past successful customer: "That makes you just like Sarah, who had a plumbing business and crushed it…"
   - Appeal to authority: "Our founder told me this earlier – can I share it?"
5. **Childlike curiosity.** Say "Huh, that's interesting…", smile, keep a mirror at your desk. Sticky note: "Keep the human #1." Time-traveller frame: you know this helps them, they don't know you yet; talk with that empathy. "You close sales by making the prospect right," not by being right.

## Objection bank (acknowledge → associate → ask)

| They say | Ask |
|---|---|
| I need to think about it | "Totally understand. What are the main variables you're weighing? What's your main concern? What would make this a no? What would it take to say yes?" |
| Timing's off / no time | "Smart that you're already thinking about implementation – our best clients do. What would make it a good time?" |
| Talk to my spouse | "Very sensible. Out of curiosity, which parts do you think they wouldn't like?" (names the real objection, keeps you in the sale) |
| I hate feature X | "Totally get it. Why not?" |
| Late in the call, weak yes | "What are you afraid of having happen?" (only with strong rapport) |

Early objection: "What's your main concern?" Late: "What are you most afraid of?"

## Ethics line (his)

Bad selling = selling an unqualified prospect, i.e. implying a result they won't get. With a qualified prospect and true claims, you have an obligation to keep asking.
