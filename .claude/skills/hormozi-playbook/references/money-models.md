# Money models (how and when you make money from one customer)

Sources: "How I Would Build a $10M Service Business" (o64cI6tebnU, roofing case study) transcript; four-offer structure from *$100M Money Models* via secondary summaries (Shortform, SuperSummary, jxnl.co). Hormozi calls the menu upsell his favourite chapter of that book, and says the book is free online.

## Four offer types

| Type | Job | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Attraction** | Get the customer in the door, often at break-even or a loss | Small repair, free/discount first service, low-cost entry tier |
| **Upsell** | Get more money now from someone who just said yes | Better version, more quantity, complementary item |
| **Downsell** | Turn a "no" into a smaller "yes" | Payment plan, fewer features, lower quantity |
| **Continuity** | Recurring revenue over time | Membership, maintenance plan, subscription |

Goal (as summarised from the book): within 30 days, one customer should generate enough gross profit to pay for acquiring and serving at least two more. Build one offer at a time; don't install all four at once.

"Getting the first sale is the hard part. Getting the next sale is significantly easier."

## Menu upsell (the four-step close)

Best for transactional sales closable in one conversation.
1. **Unsell** what they don't need: "This is fine, you've got a couple of years on this, don't worry about it." → builds trust.
2. **Prescribe** what they do need: "What you will need is this and this."
3. **A/B choice**: "Would you rather start Monday or Tuesday? Option A or B?" (a false choice that makes the yes easier).
4. **Card on file**: "Great, I'll put that on the card we already have."

Script feel: "Welcome, excited to have you. I looked at the images – gutters look great, you won't need X or Y. You will want to take care of Z. Would you rather this or this? Want that on the card on file? We already have the hood popped."

## Lead-in VSLs (video sales letters) to frame each sale

One VSL before the first sale, a different one before the upsell call. Structure:
1. Intro + promise ("in this video I'll explain X, Y, Z so you can avoid the pain you've dealt with")
2. Proof (local customers like them)
3. The plan / old way vs. new way (your named process)
4. Objections 1…n, each: belief they hold → why it's wrong → proof
Short (~7 min). It edifies the salesperson and the company before they talk.

## Continuity with credit (turns one-time into recurring)

Roofing example: "VIP" maintenance at $15–20/mo billed annually ($180–240); every $1 paid becomes $2 credit toward future repairs; includes the annual inspection. Variant: list it at $500/yr, drop to $200 for leaving a review. The point isn't the fee: it guarantees a future appointment where you can sell again, and it lifts LTV and enterprise value. Timing and wording of where it's offered decide take-rate; test them.

## Volume via the neglected product

Look for the job competitors refuse (small repairs). Systemize it, raise its average ticket (bundle everything except the big item, e.g. $1.5K → $2.5–5K), and use it as the attraction offer that "chunks up" into big tickets. High-volume, short-cycle work gives reps more reps, fewer cash-flow gaps, and forecastable volume, which in turn lets you cut costs.
