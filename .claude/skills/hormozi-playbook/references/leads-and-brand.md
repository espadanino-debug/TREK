# Leads, audience and personal brand

Sources: "How I Would Build a Personal Brand in 2027" (5rOIS7GHD5Y) transcript; door-to-door economics from o64cI6tebnU; Core Four from *$100M Leads* via secondary summaries. Note that summaries disagree on the fourth method; the 2×2 version below is the most common.

## Core Four (lead generation)

|  | One-to-one | One-to-many |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm** (know you) | Warm outreach | Post free content |
| **Cold** (don't know you) | Cold outreach | Paid ads |

**Lead getters** add capacity once a channel works: referrals, employees, agencies, affiliates. Rule: master one channel before adding another (see 1-1-1 rule).

## Make the channel math concrete

Door-to-door roofing: 1 sale per 20–50 doors, $4K average ticket, 10% commission → $400 ÷ 50 doors ≈ **$8–10 per door knocked**. "Want to make another 10 bucks? Knock that door." Translate any outreach channel into $ per action for the team, then recruit salespeople as your new avatar to scale it.

Post-job asks: Google review, then a video/reel testimonial (incentivise with the continuity discount); call past customers every 6–12 months with notes on their life. That turns into referrals and repeat work.

## Personal brand in three stages

1. **Do epic stuff first.** The "personal" part is real skill, accomplishment or volume of work. "Six months of content after a decade of experience beats a decade of content after six months of experience." No accomplishment yet? **Document volume**: 100 dates, 1,000 outreach attempts, 10,000 doors. Viewers trade attention for compressed time: "they want the Cliff Notes, but you still have to write the book."
2. **Associate, repeatedly.** A brand is just pairings repeated: visual (beard, tank top, flannel; Jobs' turtleneck) and non-visual (topics, products, people your avatar likes). Pick the avatar first: growing market, in pain, can afford it, easy to find. Associations are only good or bad *relative to your audience* (cautionary tales: Bud Light's influencer ad, Coca-Cola's AI Christmas ad; UFC pairing as the recovery). Virtuous cycle: what you say → what others say → what the customer concludes after using it. Product must fulfil the promise. Then "wait five years and don't give up."
3. **Defend the belt.** Big brands get nibbled into irrelevance. Run recurring public "belt moments" with real stakes (Red Bull space jump, Apple keynotes, his book launches and company sale). Public deadlines align the team and earn attention.

## Content angles his channel proves out

Top-performing formats (see channel-index): "If I had to start over / If I wanted X in [year], I'd do this", "N years of advice in N minutes", brutally honest advice to a younger self, "If you're [ambitious/broke/in your 20s], watch this", and live case studies (Scale or Fail: fixing a stranger's business, then showing the 90-day result).
